Canada Strong Fund Watch: can I buy it yet?

Last updated: {{ updated_h }}. We update this page when Ottawa publishes something new. General info, not advice.

Short answer: no, not yet. Target: early 2027

Status: not open. Budget 2026 (tabled Nov 5, 2026) says Canadians will be able to invest through Canada Strong Notes, sold through participating financial institutions, "with a view to launching the product in early 2027" (Canada Strong Fund Technical Backgrounder). There's no exact launch date yet.

Next to watch: the legislation to set the fund up as its own Crown corporation, and the final note terms (rates, fees, redemption rules). Until that legislation is in force, the fund is to be a subsidiary of the Canada Development Investment Corporation (CDEV).

What's been announced

  • Prime Minister Mark Carney announced the fund in April 2026 with seed funding of $25 billion over three years.
  • It's meant to invest only in Canada ("domestic-only mandate"), on a commercial basis, at arm's length from government, in projects and companies in areas like energy, infrastructure and technology.
  • New in Budget 2026: the retail product is a fund-linked principal protected note called a Canada Strong Note. Per the budget, it would have set terms (examples given: 5, 7 or 10 years), a base coupon rate, principal protected at maturity, and extra returns tied to how the fund performs.
  • The fund is to have its own board and CEO. The government says it will table legislation to make it a stand-alone Crown corporation.

Sources: Budget 2026 Canada Strong Fund Technical Backgrounder; Budget 2026: Key Measures (Department of Finance Canada, Nov 5, 2026).

"Principal protected" here is the budget's description of the planned product. Final terms will be in the official offering documents.

⚠️ Scam warning

There is no way to buy into the Canada Strong Fund or Canada Strong Notes today. If anyone offers you "early access", a "pre-sale" or guaranteed returns from it, by ad, text, email, phone or social media, treat it as a scam. Don't send money or personal details.

When it does open, the details will come from the Government of Canada (canada.ca). Report suspected fraud to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
